You are annotating short sentences according to a 7-tier taxonomy of states of mind.

THE 7 TIERS
The tiers are ordered from highly contracted, self-destructive states to expansive, integrative states:

1. Collapse (Low Awareness / Self-Destructive):
   Highly contracted and self-destructive; apathy, despair.
   Ignorance, self-blame, despair, apathy, disintegrated modes of experience.

2. Striving (Scarcity and Attachment):
   Contracted and striving; fear, manipulation.
   Fear, craving, insecurity, anxiety, survival-oriented thinking.

3. Conflict (Ego and Opposition):
   Defensive; ego, anger.
   Anger, hostility, dominance, control, power struggles.

4. Activation (Energy Mobilization):
   Activated and transitional; accepting, courage.
   Courage, resolve, neutrality, acceptance, behavioral readiness.

5. Growth (Inner Reorganization):
   Constructive and growth-oriented; relaxed, healing.
   Openness, forgiveness, transformation, contribution, learning.

6. Clarity (Cognitive Integration):
   Integrated and harmonious; clarity, reasoning.
   Reasoning, abstraction, understanding, coherence, meaning.

7. Unity (Non-Dual Integration):
   Expansive and unifying; grace, wisdom.
   Compassion, joy, peace, surrender, wholeness.

Each tier represents a mode of organization of experience rather than merely emotional valence.

ABOUT THE SENTENCES
The sentences use predominantly first-person, introspective, or experiential language; higher-tier sentences tend to adopt more contemplative or aphoristic phrasing that reflects increasingly abstract or integrative states. They rely only in a limited way on explicit emotion words (e.g., "happy", "sad", "angry"), so judge the underlying stance or mode of experience, not just the surface emotion word.

For example, rehearsing every conversation in advance so as never to be caught off guard reflects a striving-oriented stance without any explicit emotion word, and signing up for something despite nerves reflects readiness and transition rather than a direct expression of sentiment.
