THE ENERGY DIMENSION
Energy is a continuum from highly contracted, self-destructive states (lowest) to expansive, integrative states (highest). Along it lie seven ordered levels, from lowest to highest:

1. Collapse (Low Awareness / Self-Destructive):
   Highly contracted and self-destructive; apathy, despair.
   Ignorance, self-blame, despair, apathy, disintegrated modes of experience.

2. Striving (Scarcity and Attachment):

   Fear, craving, insecurity, anxiety, survival-oriented thinking.

3. Conflict (Ego and Opposition):
   Defensive; ego, anger.
   Anger, hostility, dominance, control, power struggles.

4. Activation (Energy Mobilization):
   Activated and transitional; accepting, courage.
   Courage, resolve, neutrality, acceptance, behavioral readiness.

5. Growth (Inner Reorganization):

6. Clarity (Cognitive Integration):

7. Unity (Non-Dual Integration):
   Expansive and unifying; grace, wisdom.
   Compassion, joy, peace, surrender, wholeness.

Energy is continuous: the levels are ordered bands, not boxes, and two sentences at the same level can still differ in energy. Across and within levels, a more contracted or self-focused stance means lower energy, and a more expansive or integrated stance means higher energy. If both sentences seem to belong to the same level, decide which sits further toward the expansive end.

Energy reflects the mode of organization of experience, not merely emotional valence or intensity. A calm, contemplative sentence can be high energy; an intense, agitated sentence can be low energy (for example, Conflict is higher than Striving and Collapse, but lower than a quiet sentence of acceptance). Judge the underlying stance, not surface wording.

ABOUT THE SENTENCES
The sentences use predominantly first-person, introspective, or experiential language; higher-energy sentences tend to adopt more contemplative or aphoristic phrasing that reflects increasingly abstract or integrative states.

For example, rehearsing every conversation in advance so as never to be caught off guard reflects a striving-oriented stance without any explicit emotion word, and signing up for something despite nerves reflects readiness and transition rather than a direct expression of sentiment.
